# Financial Modeling & Pricing Strategy for Growth

Marketing does not operate in a vacuum. Being the "best marketer in the world" means understanding that **acquisition strategy is dictated by unit economics and pricing power.** 

At Palencia Diamonds, our high-AOV (Average Order Value) structure fundamentally changes how we measure success. We cannot afford the luxury of low-margin volume; we must optimize for **Contribution Margin Dollars**, not just top-line revenue or ROAS.

---

## 1. The Palencia Unit Economic Engine

To dictate marketing budgets and target acquisition costs, we must first break down the unit economics of a standard Palencia Diamond engagement ring.

### The Breakdown (Illustrative)
*   **Average Order Value (AOV):** $15,000
*   **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** $7,500 (Diamond, Setting, Labor, Packaging)
*   **Gross Margin:** 50% ($7,500)
*   **Variable Operational Costs (Fulfillment, Shipping, Ins, Merchant Fees):** $500
*   **Contribution Margin 1 (CM1):** $7,000 (46.6%)

**The Marketing Mandate:**
Our CM1 ($7,000) is the absolute maximum we can spend to acquire a customer (CPA) and break even on the first purchase. 

### Setting the Target CPA (tCPA)
We do not optimize for ROAS directly. We optimize for a Target CPA that delivers our required profitability.

If the business mandates a 20% Net Profit Margin ($3,000) on a $15,000 order, our math is:
`Target CPA = CM1 - Required Profit`
`Target CPA = $7,000 - $3,000 = $4,000`

Therefore, the marketing engine is given a strict mandate: **Acquire customers at a Blended CPA of $4,000 or lower.**

### Translating tCPA to Target MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio)
`Target MER = AOV / Target CPA`
`Target MER = $15,000 / $4,000 = 3.75x`

Our daily operational pulse is a **3.75x MER**. If we hit 3.75x, the business is perfectly hitting its profit targets.

## 2. Pricing Strategy as a Marketing Lever

In fine jewelry, pricing is not just a financial output; it is a psychological marketing input.

### The "Veblen Good" Reality
Luxury jewelry exhibits characteristics of a Veblen good—where demand increases as price increases, because price signals quality and exclusivity. 
*   **Discounting is Poison:** We do not run 20% off sitewide sales. It degrades the brand equity of a $15,000 diamond. 
*   **Price Anchoring:** We lead with our highest-tier products in prospecting ads to establish premium anchoring, making the $15,000 ring feel accessible compared to a $40,000 halo piece.

### Dynamic Pricing & Margin Protection
Our pricing strategy must absorb fluctuating diamond wholesale prices (Rapaport pricing) and gold commodities without shocking the consumer.

1.  **Fixed Premium on Settings:** The margin on the setting (gold/platinum) is fixed and high (70%+).
2.  **Dynamic Margin on Stones:** The margin on the center diamond fluctuates. We accept lower margins on highly competitive, commoditized cuts (e.g., 1.5ct Round H/VS2) to win the click, and make up the margin on the setting and add-ons (insurance, warranties).

## 3. LTV : CAC (Lifetime Value to Customer Acquisition Cost)

Engagement rings are traditionally low-frequency purchases. However, Palencia models a 10-year LTV window based on lifecycle marketing (Anniversaries, push presents, upgrades).

*   **Year 1 AOV (The Ring):** $15,000
*   **Year 3-10 LTV (Bands, Necklaces, Earrings):** +$5,000
*   **Total LTV:** $20,000

While our day-one Target CPA is $4,000 (Break-even on CM1), our LTV approach allows us to "float" a higher initial acquisition cost if cash flow permits, knowing the cohort will mature to profitability.

**The Golden Ratio Target:**
We aim for an **LTV:CAC ratio of > 3:1**.
If LTV is $20,000 (Revenue) resulting in $10,000 (CM1 over 10 years), and CAC is $4,000, our true CM-based LTV:CAC is 2.5:1. 

*(Note: In luxury e-commerce, a 2.5:1 ratio on Contribution Margin is highly sustainable, unlike SaaS where 3:1 on Gross Margin is standard).*

## 4. The Marketing "P&L" Meeting (Weekly SOP)

The marketing team does not just report on clicks and CPCs. They act as capital allocators.

Every Monday, Growth Ops leads the Marketing P&L Review:

1.  **The MER Check:** Did we hit the 3.75x MER target? If not, why?
2.  **The Marginal ROAS Check (from MMM):** Where is the next $10,000 best spent? Are we saturating Meta? 
3.  **The Inventory/Margin Sync:** Merchandising reports that Oval cuts are currently yielding higher margins due to supply chain wins. Marketing immediately shifts creative budget to push Oval styles to capitalize on the higher CM%.

## Summary

The best marketers think like CFOs. By anchoring our media buying logic (MMM), creative testing, and CRO efforts directly to Contribution Margin and Target CPA, the Palencia marketing engine guarantees profitable growth, immune to the vanity metrics of the ad platforms.
