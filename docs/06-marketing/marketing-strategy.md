## 1. Purpose & scope

This document is the master plan for demand generation and customer growth at Palencia Diamonds. It defines *who* we are trying to reach, *what* we say to them, *how* the money is allocated across channels, and *how we measure whether it worked*.

It sits above the channel- and discipline-specific playbooks:

- **What we say and stand for** → [Brand Positioning](../01-brand/brand-positioning.md)
- **How we get found and educate** → [Content & SEO](content-and-seo.md)
- **Where we show up and spend** → [Channels & Social](channels-and-social.md), with per-platform depth in the [Instagram](playbooks/instagram.md), [Facebook & Meta Ads](playbooks/facebook.md), [YouTube](playbooks/youtube.md), [TikTok](playbooks/tiktok.md), and [Google Ads](playbooks/google-ads.md) playbooks
- **How we retain and delight** → [CRM & Clienteling](../04-sales-cx/crm-and-clienteling.md)

Everything here serves one measurable end from our vision: **the percentage of customers who would buy from us again and send us their family and friends.**

## 2. The strategic problem we are solving

A diamond purchase is unlike almost anything else we buy:

| Trait of the purchase | What it means for marketing |
|---|---|
| **High price (high AOV)** | Buyers research for weeks or months; one-click impulse rarely applies. |
| **High emotional stakes** | An engagement ring is tied to one of life's biggest moments — fear of "getting it wrong" is the dominant emotion. |
| **Low frequency / rare repeat** | Most engagement-ring buyers buy once. Lifetime value is built through *adjacent* purchases and *referrals*, not repeat rings. |
| **Information asymmetry** | Buyers fear being fooled on grade, price, or authenticity. Trust is the true currency. |
| **Commoditized inputs** | A 1.00ct G VS1 round is gradeable and comparable everywhere — so we compete on **trust, service, and honesty**, not on owning a unique stone. |

**Strategic implication:** We do not "sell diamonds." We *remove fear and build confidence* so a high-stakes buyer chooses us. Every tactic in this document should reduce perceived risk (certification, transparent pricing, generous service) and deepen trust.

## 3. Positioning-driven messaging

Our positioning (see [Brand Positioning](../01-brand/brand-positioning.md)) resolves to three message pillars that map directly to our brand promise.

| Pillar | Proof we lead with | Primary buyer fear it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Certified** — *"Certified diamonds"* | Independent GIA/IGI report on every center stone | "How do I know it's really what they say?" |
| **Handcrafted** — *"Handcrafted jewelry"* | Hand-set, QC-inspected pieces; custom design | "Is this mass-produced or made with care?" |
| **Honest** — *"Honest prices"* | Transparent, defensible pricing; no markup theater | "Am I being overcharged / fooled?" |

**Messaging rules of thumb:**
- Lead with the fear we relieve, then the proof. ("Nervous about the price? Here's exactly what you're paying for.")
- Show the certificate. Never make a grade claim we can't document.
- Warm, plain, precise language — never luxury-jargon or pressure. See [Voice & Tone](../01-brand/voice-and-tone.md).
- Honesty includes trade-offs: we will tell a buyer when a smaller, cleaner stone beats a bigger, included one.

## 5. The customer journey & funnel

A long buying cycle needs a funnel that nurtures, not just captures. We manage four stages.

```
AWARENESS         CONSIDERATION        PURCHASE           LOYALTY / ADVOCACY
(discover us)     (evaluate & trust)   (buy with          (return & refer)
                                       confidence)
   │                   │                    │                     │
 Social, SEO,      Buying guides,       Product pages,      Post-purchase care,
 Pinterest,        4Cs education,       reviews, live      warranty, reviews,
 influencers,      email nurture,       chat, financing,    referral, clienteling,
 PR, paid social   retargeting,         free insured        band/upgrade offers
                   comparison, reviews  shipping, easy
                                        returns
```

### Stage goals, tactics, and the "one metric that matters"

| Stage | Buyer question | Our job | Lead channels | Primary KPI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | "Who is this?" | Get discovered by the right people | Organic/paid social, SEO, Pinterest, influencers, PR | Qualified new-visitor volume; assisted reach |
| **Consideration** | "Can I trust them, and is this right for me?" | Educate, prove certification & honest pricing, capture the email | Content/SEO, email nurture, retargeting, reviews | Email capture rate; return-visit rate |
| **Purchase** | "Am I making a safe, smart decision?" | Remove final friction and fear | Product pages, reviews, chat/consult, financing, guarantees | Conversion rate; AOV |
| **Loyalty / Advocacy** | "Was I right to trust them?" | Over-deliver, then invite referral & repeat | Post-purchase email/SMS, clienteling, referral, review asks | Repeat/referral rate; review volume & rating; NPS |

### Journey nuance for engagement rings (the long cycle)

The ring cycle commonly runs **weeks to several months**. Marketing must be patient and present the whole time.

- **Weeks 0–2 — "Am I ready / where do I start?"** Education-first content; capture email early with a buying guide.
- **Weeks 2–8 — "What do I actually want / can I afford?"** Nurture email, 4Cs education, style quizzes, budget framing, retargeting.
- **Weeks 6–12+ — "Who do I buy from?"** Reviews, certification proof, guarantees, live consultation, financing.
- **Post-purchase — "Did I choose well?"** Reassurance, care, wedding-band cross-sell, referral.

> **Implication:** Optimize for *email capture and nurture*, not last-click conversion. A 20-minute session that ends with a captured email and a downloaded guide is a *win*, even with no purchase that day.

## 6. Budget allocation across channels

We allocate to balance **demand capture** (people already looking — high intent, efficient) and **demand creation** (building future pipeline — brand, education, social). For a long-cycle, high-AOV product, under-investing in capture wastes created demand; under-investing in creation starves the top of the funnel.

**Allocation principles:**
1. **Protect capture first.** Never let created demand leak to competitors because we under-bid on high-intent queries.
2. **Fund the compounding assets** (SEO, email list, reviews) even when they don't attribute last-click — they lower blended CAC over time.
3. **Shift 10–15% into a "test" pool** each quarter for new channels/creatives; kill losers fast.
4. **Concentrate spend into seasonal peaks** (Section 8) rather than spreading evenly.

## 7. CAC, LTV & payback thinking

Because rings rarely repeat, we cannot rely on classic repeat-purchase LTV. We think in **three LTV layers**:

| LTV layer | Source of value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1. First order** | The ring / piece itself (high AOV) | Must often cover CAC on its own or near it |
| **2. Adjacent purchases** | Wedding bands, anniversary gifts, upgrades, self-purchase | Extends value from a single customer over years |
| **3. Referral value** | Friends/family they send us (our vision metric) | Often the largest hidden LTV in a referral-driven, once-in-a-lifetime category |

**Because referral is a real LTV layer, referral and review generation are treated as acquisition channels, not afterthoughts.** A delighted customer who sends two friends effectively halves our CAC.

## 8. Seasonality & the marketing calendar

Demand is highly seasonal. We plan creative, inventory readiness, and budget concentration around these peaks.

| Season / moment | Window | Primary buyer | Marketing focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engagement season** | Late Nov – Feb (peak around holidays, Valentine's, "proposal season") | Ring buyer | Full-funnel push; guides, financing, deadline messaging |
| **Valentine's Day** | Late Jan – Feb 14 | Gift buyer, ring buyer | Gift guides, shipping deadlines, romantic creative |
| **Mother's Day** | Apr – early May | Gift buyer, self-purchaser | Pendants, earrings, "for Mom" curation |
| **Wedding season** | May – Sep | Ring→band buyers, gifts | Wedding bands, bridal party gifts, anniversary |
| **Graduation / Father's Day** | May – Jun | Gift buyer | Milestone gifting, secondary push |
| **Holiday / December** | Nov – Dec | Gift buyer + engagements | Biggest revenue window; gift + proposal dual message |
| **Anniversary / evergreen** | Year-round | Self & gift | Always-on capture, upgrades, self-purchase |

> **Lead-time rule:** SEO and content for a season must ship **8–12 weeks early** to rank in time; paid budgets ramp **2–4 weeks** before peak. Never launch a seasonal guide the week of the holiday.

**Shipping-deadline discipline:** Because we ship free, insured, signature-required goods, every gifting peak needs a published "order by" date and countdown messaging across email/SMS and site banners. Coordinate with [Operations & Fulfillment](../03-operations/).

## 10. Operating principles (how we make trade-offs)

1. **Trust before conversion.** If a tactic would win a sale but dent trust (pressure, hidden fees, inflated grades), we don't run it. Honesty is the product.
2. **Capture the email, earn the cycle.** For a months-long decision, an owned audience beats a rented one.
3. **Compounding over quick hits.** Fund SEO, reviews, and referral even when last-click can't see them.
4. **Concentrate on peaks.** Spend and creative energy follow the seasonal calendar.
5. **Measure incrementality, not vanity.** Ask "did this create a sale that wouldn't have happened?" — not "did it get clicks?"
6. **One brand, everywhere.** Every channel expresses the same warm, honest, precise voice — see [Brand Guidelines](../01-brand/brand-guidelines.md).
