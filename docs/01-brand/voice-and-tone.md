# Voice & Tone

> How Palencia Diamonds sounds — the words we use, the words we avoid, and how our tone shifts with the moment while our voice stays the same.

## 1. Voice vs. tone

**Voice** is who we are — it never changes. **Tone** is how we adapt that voice to the moment — it changes with context. A celebratory launch and an apology for a delayed ring should sound like the same company, but not the same sentence.

## 2. Voice principles

Our voice rests on four principles, drawn directly from our values.

### Principle 1 — Clear over clever
We say the thing plainly. In a category built on confusion — obscure grades, mystery markups — clarity is a competitive advantage and a form of respect.
> Write the sentence a smart friend would understand on the first read.

### Principle 2 — Warm and human
We sell into life's biggest moments. We sound like a person who cares, not a brand performing care. Warmth without gush.

### Principle 3 — Honest and precise
We tell the truth about grades, prices, trade-offs, and timelines — and we get the details exactly right. In a certified-diamond business, precision *is* the brand. A misquoted carat weight is not a typo; it's a broken promise.

### Principle 4 — Confident, never pushy
We are the expert in the room, but the customer's moment is theirs. We guide; we never pressure. No urgency tricks, no upselling, no "act now."

## 3. Tone shifts by context

The voice is constant; the tone dial moves. Here is where it should sit.

| Context | Tone | Dial settings | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales / product page** | Warm, informative, quietly confident | Precise ↑, celebratory ↕, salesy ↓↓ | Hype, false urgency, overselling a grade |
| **Educational content** | Patient, expert, generous | Clear ↑↑, warm ↑ | Condescension, jargon without explanation |
| **Order & shipping updates** | Reassuring, specific, prompt | Precise ↑↑, warm ↑ | Vagueness, hiding a delay |
| **Customer-service apology** | Accountable, warm, generous | Empathy ↑↑, honesty ↑↑, defensiveness ↓↓ | Excuses, policy-hiding, minimizing |
| **Social media** | Human, real, understated | Warm ↑, celebratory ↑, hype ↓ | Trend-chasing, pressure, overclaiming |
| **Custom / consultative** | Collaborative, expert, unhurried | Warmth ↑, patience ↑↑ | Rushing the customer, pushing spend |

## 4. How we talk about diamonds honestly

This is the heart of our verbal identity. How we describe a stone is where trust is won or lost.

- **Use exact, standard terminology.** The 4Cs (cut, color, clarity, carat), GIA/IGI grades, and hallmarks — stated accurately, never rounded up.
- **Cite the certificate, not our opinion.** "This stone is graded VS1 by GIA (report #…)" — not "flawless-looking" or "practically perfect."
- **Name trade-offs plainly.** If a slightly lower color grade saves the customer money with no visible difference, say so. Honesty about trade-offs is a feature.
- **Never imply a grade we can't back.** No "premium quality," "top-grade," or invented tiers that aren't on the report.
- **Explain, don't dazzle.** Help the customer understand what they're buying; a confident buyer is a loyal one.

## 5. Words we use / words we avoid

| We use | We avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Independently certified," "GIA/IGI report" | "Certified by us," "guaranteed authentic" | Third-party proof is the point |
| "Transparent pricing," "what you're paying for" | "Lowest price," "unbeatable deal," "wholesale" | We compete on honesty, not price wars |
| "Handcrafted," "hand-set," "inspected" | "Luxury," "exclusive," "premium" (as empty adjectives) | Specific and true beats grand and vague |
| "Graded VS1," "F color," exact specs | "Flawless-looking," "top quality," "perfect" | Grades are defined; adjectives aren't |
| "We got this wrong. Here's how we'll fix it." | "We apologize for any inconvenience" | Real accountability, not a formula |
| "Complimentary resizing," "insured shipping" | "Free stuff," "special offer" | Service is a promise, not a promotion |
| "When you're ready," "take your time" | "Act now," "limited time," "don't miss out" | The customer's moment, no pressure |
| "Responsibly sourced," "conflict-free, documented" | "Ethical" (unqualified), "sustainable" (unproven) | Only claims we can back |

## 6. Grammar & style conventions

| Convention | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Person** | Second person to the customer ("your ring"), first-person plural for us ("we") |
| **Voice** | Active voice ("We inspect every piece"), not passive ("Every piece is inspected") — except where passive is genuinely clearer |
| **Contractions** | Yes — they keep us warm and human ("we'll," "you're") |
| **Sentence length** | Short and varied. When in doubt, split it. |
| **Numbers** | Spell out one through nine in prose; use numerals for specs, carats, prices, and measurements (e.g., "1.2 ct," "VS1," "$3,400") |
| **The 4Cs** | Lowercase "cut, color, clarity, carat"; "4Cs" as the shorthand |
| **Lab names** | "GIA" and "IGI" always uppercase; spell out on first mention if the audience is new to certification |
| **Tagline** | Exact, three clauses, each capitalized and period-terminated |
| **Oxford comma** | Yes |
| **Emoji** | Sparing to none in brand copy; acceptable in social where it reads as genuinely human, never as hype |

## 7. Before / after examples

| Context | Before (off-brand) | After (Palencia) |
|---|---|---|
| Product description | "This absolutely flawless, premium diamond is a once-in-a-lifetime stunner!" | "A 1.20 ct round diamond, graded F color / VS1 clarity by GIA (report #…), hand-set in platinum." |
| Pricing | "Was $5,999 — NOW $3,999! Biggest sale ever!" | "$3,999. Here's exactly what that price reflects: the stone, the setting, the metal, and our service." |
| Urgency | "Only 2 left — order in the next 10 minutes!" | "Made to order. We'll confirm your timeline before anything is set." |
| Shipping delay | "Your order has been delayed. We apologize for any inconvenience." | "Your ring will ship two days later than we promised — QC caught a setting detail we want to get right. New ship date: [date]. Here's a tracking link the moment it moves." |
| Service apology | "Per our policy, returns must be within 30 days." | "We got this wrong, and we'll make it right. Here are your options, and I'll handle the details for you." |
| Certification | "Our diamonds are the highest quality, guaranteed." | "Every center stone comes with an independent GIA or IGI report you can verify yourself." |
| Social post | "🔥 DROP ALERT 🔥 You NEED this ring!! Link in bio!!!" | "A three-stone setting, hand-set this week. Swipe for the certificate. Questions about the stones? Ask us anything." |

## 8. Quick self-check

Before publishing, ask:

- [ ] Would I say this to a close friend buying their engagement ring?
- [ ] Is every claim true and verifiable?
- [ ] Is the tone right for this moment (celebrate, reassure, or apologize)?
- [ ] Did I remove all pressure, hype, and false urgency?
- [ ] Are the diamond specs exact and cited to the certificate?
- [ ] Is it clear on the first read?
