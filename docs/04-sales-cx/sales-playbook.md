## 1. What this playbook is (and isn't)

This is the shared method every Palencia advisor uses to help a customer choose the right piece. It is **not** a script to push a sale. Our value is explicit: *the customer's moment, not our transaction.* A high-pressure tactic is a values violation, not a shortcut.

Our job is to make the customer **more informed and more confident** than when they arrived — even if that means recommending they spend less, wait, or buy nothing today. Done right, that is exactly what earns the sale, the referral, and the return customer.

**The Palencia advisor's promise to the customer:**

- We will tell you the truth about every stone, price, and trade-off.
- We will never rush you. A ring is forever; a decision can take a week.
- We will show you options at, below, and above your budget — and respect the one you choose.
- We will be here for the life of the piece, not just until checkout.

## 2. The consultative sales arc

We follow five stages. They are a rhythm, not a checklist to race through. A customer may enter at any stage and move back and forth.

| Stage | Goal | Advisor mindset |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Understand** | Learn the moment, budget, timeline, and taste | Curiosity, not qualifying |
| **2. Educate** | Give honest 4Cs literacy so they can decide well | Teacher, not seller |
| **3. Guide** | Narrow stone + setting to a confident shortlist | Trusted expert |
| **4. Reassure** | Dissolve hesitation with facts and guarantees | Calm, patient |
| **5. Confirm & follow up** | Make ordering easy; stay in touch | Concierge |

## 3. Stage 1 — Understand the customer's moment

Before any product talk, understand the human. Most engagement-ring buyers have never bought a diamond before and are quietly anxious about overpaying or "getting it wrong."

### Discovery-question checklist

Use as a natural conversation, not an interrogation. Aim for the ✅ items every time; the others as relevant.

- [ ] ✅ **The occasion** — "What's the occasion you're shopping for?" (engagement, anniversary, wedding band, gift, self-purchase)
- [ ] ✅ **The person** — "Tell me about the person who'll wear it — their style, what they gravitate toward."
- [ ] ✅ **Timeline** — "Is there a date you're working toward?" (proposal, anniversary, holiday)
- [ ] ✅ **Budget comfort** — asked gently (see script below)
- [ ] **Style cues** — metal color they wear now, gold vs. platinum, classic vs. modern, subtle vs. statement
- [ ] **Lifestyle** — hands-on job, active, sensitivity to certain metals (affects durability/setting advice)
- [ ] **Ring size** — known? Or do we need a discreet sizing plan?
- [ ] **Natural vs. lab-grown** — any existing preference or values around this?
- [ ] **Decision process** — "Are you choosing this on your own, or together?"
- [ ] **What "getting it right" means to them** — the single most useful question

### Script — opening a chat/email warmly

> "Congratulations — this is a wonderful thing to be shopping for. I'd love to help you get it right. To point you to the best options, can I ask a couple of quick things about who it's for and the moment you have in mind? No pressure at all — I'm here to make this easier, not to sell you anything you're not sure about."

### Script — asking about budget without pressure

Budget is sensitive. Frame it as **serving them**, never qualifying them.

> "So I show you the right options and don't waste your time on pieces that aren't a fit — do you have a range in mind you'd like me to work within? There's no right number, and there are beautiful pieces at every level. If you'd rather I show you a spread and let the pieces speak, that works too."

If they're unsure of budget: offer a range of three price points and let the stones make the case.

## 4. Stage 2 — Educate on the 4Cs, honestly

Our differentiator is honesty. We teach customers to be **good buyers**, which sometimes means steering them *away* from spending more. See the full reference in [Diamond Education — The 4Cs](../09-product/diamond-education-4cs.md); below is how we *talk about* it.

### The one-minute honest 4Cs

| C | What we tell customers plainly | The honest steer |
|---|---|---|
| **Cut** | How well the diamond is faceted; the biggest driver of sparkle | "If you spend up on one thing, spend on cut. It's what people actually see." |
| **Color** | How colorless the stone is (D–Z) | "Near-colorless (G–I) usually looks white face-up and costs far less than D–F. You often can't tell the difference set in a ring." |
| **Clarity** | Internal/surface characteristics | "Most inclusions are invisible without magnification. An 'eye-clean' VS or good SI can look identical to flawless for a fraction of the price." |
| **Carat** | Weight, not size | "Weight jumps in price at round numbers (1.0, 1.5, 2.0ct). A 0.90ct can look the same as a 1.0ct and cost noticeably less." |

### Script — the honest trade-off

> "Here's how I'd think about your budget. If it were me, I'd prioritize cut, keep color in the near-colorless range, and choose an eye-clean clarity grade rather than paying for perfection no one can see. That gets you the most beautiful stone for your money — not the biggest spec sheet. Want me to show you two stones side by side so you can see exactly what I mean?"

**Never do this:** oversell a grade, imply a higher grade is "better for her," or use a customer's inexperience to upsell. That breaks our first value and our brand.

## 5. Stage 3 — Guide stone & setting selection

Move from education to a **confident shortlist** of two or three options.

### Method

1. **Anchor on the priority** the customer revealed (sparkle, size, a specific shape, a heirloom feel).
2. **Present 2–3 curated options**, not an overwhelming grid. Each with its certificate.
3. **Explain the "why"** for each: what it optimizes and what it trades off.
4. **Always include one option below budget** — it proves we're on their side.
5. **Pair stone with setting**: shape → setting styles that flatter it; lifestyle → durability (e.g., recommend bezel/lower-profile for active hands, note that pavé needs occasional care).

### Script — presenting a shortlist

> "Based on what you told me, I pulled three I'd genuinely recommend. The first maximizes sparkle and comes in a touch under your budget. The second is a half-carat larger for the same money because it's a near-colorless G instead of a D — you truly can't see the difference face-up. The third is the splurge if size is the priority. All three are GIA-certified and I'll send the reports. Which direction feels most like *her*?"

### Every stone ships with proof

Reinforce, naturally, that every center stone carries an independent **GIA or IGI** report — this is a fact, not a sales line. It's why customers can trust the choice.

## 6. Stage 4 — Handling common questions

Answer with education, never defensiveness. Below are the recurring ones with honest, on-brand responses.

### Natural vs. lab-grown

> "Both are real diamonds — chemically and optically identical. Natural diamonds formed over billions of years and hold a rarity and resale character that many people value symbolically. Lab-grown are created in weeks, cost significantly less for the same look, and let you get a larger or higher-grade stone for the budget. Neither is 'better' — it's about what the two of you value. I'm happy to show the same design both ways so you can compare price and size directly."

- We do **not** disparage either category.

That report is graded by a third party, not by us, so you're not taking our word for it. I'll send you the certificate for any stone you're considering, and you can verify it directly on the lab's website using the report number."

> "Very common — and easy to solve. We offer complimentary resizing where the design allows, so you can order close and we'll adjust it. I can also walk you through a few discreet ways to find her size before you order. Either way, you're covered."

### "Is this a good price?"

> "Fair question, and you should ask it. Our pricing is transparent — you're paying for the certified stone and the handcraft, not a traditional retail markup. Here's the certificate so you can compare the exact specs anywhere. I'd rather you feel confident than rushed."

### Objection-handling principles

| Hesitation | Respond with | Never |
|---|---|---|
| "It's a lot of money." | Empathy + below-budget options + guarantees | Guilt or urgency |
| "I need to think about it." | "Absolutely — it should be a sure thing." Offer to hold notes/shortlist. | Fake scarcity |
| "I found it cheaper elsewhere." | Compare exact certified specs honestly | Bad-mouthing competitors |
| "What if she doesn't like it?" | Return/exchange + custom + resizing reassurance | Pressure to commit |

## 7. Stage 5 — Custom-design handoff

When a customer wants something made-to-order, we transition smoothly from advisor to design partner.

### When to route to custom

- The customer wants a specific combination we don't stock (stone + setting + metal + carat).
- They're recreating or honoring an heirloom.
- They want a truly one-of-a-kind piece.

### Handoff steps

1. **Set expectations plainly** — timeline, that a custom piece is made-to-order, and how revisions/approvals work.
2. **Capture the brief** — inspiration images, must-haves, budget, deadline, ring size.
3. **Introduce the design process** and confirm the customer knows the next contact and rough timeline.
4. **Log everything in the CRM** so nothing is lost in the handoff. See [CRM & Clienteling](crm-and-clienteling.md).

### Script — introducing custom

> "What you're describing is a perfect fit for our custom design. We'll design it around exactly what you want — stone, metal, carat, and setting — and you'll approve the design before anything is made. It typically takes `TODO: [confirm standard custom lead time]`, so let's make sure that fits your timeline. Shall I start your design brief?"

Full process: [Custom Design Workflow](../03-operations/custom-design-workflow.md).

## 8. Closing with confidence — and following up

Closing is not "getting the yes." It's **making a confident decision easy to act on** and being present afterward.

### At the moment of decision

- Summarize *their* reasoning back to them ("You chose this because…") so they own the decision.
- Restate the guarantees that reduce risk: free insured shipping, resizing, defect coverage, returns.
- Make ordering frictionless; offer to place the order together on a call/video if helpful.
- **Never** manufacture urgency. If a genuine constraint exists (a specific stone is one-of-a-kind, or a deadline is tight), state it factually.

### Script — a no-pressure close

> "I think you've landed on a beautiful choice, and for good reasons. Whenever you're ready, I can have it on its way to you — shipped free, fully insured, with signature confirmation. And if anything about the fit or the moment isn't perfect when it arrives, we'll make it right. Would you like to go ahead, or is there anything still on your mind I can help with?"

### If they're not ready

> "Totally understandable — this should feel like a sure thing. I'll keep your shortlist and notes on file so nothing's lost, and I'm here whenever you want to pick it back up. No rush at all."

### Follow-up cadence (post-conversation, pre-purchase)

| When | Action | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Send certificates + shortlist summary | Helpful recap |
| Day 2–3 | Gentle check-in *only if they engaged* | "Any questions I can answer?" |
| Day 7 | One value-add note (education, not push) | Optional |
| After that | Stop unless they re-engage | Respectful silence |

We follow up to **serve**, not to nag. Two unanswered, respectful touches is the ceiling before we let it rest. Detailed cadences live in [CRM & Clienteling](crm-and-clienteling.md).

## 9. What great looks like

A Palencia sales interaction is successful when the customer would say:

- "They told me the truth, even when it saved me money."
- "I never felt pushed."
- "I understand what I bought and why."
- "I'd send my best friend to them."

That is the whole strategy. Trust compounds; pressure doesn't.
