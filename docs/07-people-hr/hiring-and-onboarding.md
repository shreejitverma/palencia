## 1. What we hire for

We hire for **values first, craft close behind, and coachability throughout.** A brilliant setter who cuts corners on honesty is a worse hire than a solid setter who tells the truth every time. In a certified-diamond business, trust is the product — so it's the first thing we screen for.

Every hire should strengthen at least one of these:
- **Craft & expertise** — gemology, setting, QC, or domain skill that raises our quality bar.
- **Customer trust** — the judgment and warmth to be part of someone's biggest moment.
- **Integrity under pressure** — will do the honest, precise thing when a deadline or a discrepancy makes it inconvenient.

## 2. Role scoping (before we post)

Don't open a role until the scope is clear. The hiring manager and People & Culture agree on:

| Element | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | What outcome does this role own? Which department KPI does it move? |
| **Level & fit in org** | Where does it sit? See [Roles & Responsibilities](../02-governance/roles-and-responsibilities.md). |
| **Must-have skills** | Non-negotiable craft/credentials (e.g., GG credential, bench experience, AML familiarity). |
| **Nice-to-haves** | Skills we can train post-hire. |
| **Values signals** | The specific behaviors this role must demonstrate. |
| **On-site vs. remote** | Does the role touch physical inventory? If so, it's on-site (see the [Employee Handbook](employee-handbook.md) §4). |
| **Comp range** | Defensible band agreed before sourcing, applied consistently. |
| **Success at 90 days** | What "working out" looks like, written down. |

## 4. Structured interview process

Every candidate for a given role goes through the **same** structured process and is scored against the **same** criteria. This is fairer, more predictive, and more defensible than gut-feel interviewing.

| Stage | Purpose | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Application review** | Screen against must-haves, consistently. | Recruiter / hiring manager |
| **2. Recruiter screen** | Motivation, logistics, comp expectations, values signal. | People & Culture |
| **3. Craft assessment** | Prove the skill: bench test for setters, grading exercise for gemologists, mock consultation for CX, case for marketing/finance. | Hiring manager + peer |
| **4. Values-based interview** | Behavioral, evidence-based (see §5). | Cross-functional panel |
| **5. Team & culture-add** | Two-way fit; candidate meets future teammates. | Team |
| **6. Final / hiring-manager decision** | Synthesis against the scorecard. | Hiring manager |

**Ground rules:** a written scorecard per interviewer, submitted before debriefs; no leading or discriminatory questions (`[bracketed] TODO: counsel-reviewed do-not-ask list per jurisdiction`); consistent questions across candidates; debrief on evidence, not "vibes."

### Craft assessment examples
- **Setter / jeweler:** timed, supervised bench exercise on scrap/CZ; assess prong work, finish, cleanliness, and care with materials — never with client stones.
- **Gemologist:** grade a set of known stones against the 4Cs and reconcile to the lab report — see [Diamond Education: The 4Cs](../09-product/diamond-education-4cs.md).
- **Sales/CX:** role-play a nervous first-time engagement-ring buyer; look for honesty, patience, zero pressure.
- **QC:** inspect prepared pieces and catch seeded defects.

## 5. Values-based interviewing

Ask for **past behavior**, not hypotheticals ("Tell me about a time…"). Probe for the situation, the action they personally took, and the result. Map each value to a question.

| Value | Sample behavioral prompt | Green flag | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Honesty is the product** | "Tell me about a time telling the truth cost you a sale or made your day harder." | Chose candor even at a cost | Rationalizes shading the truth |
| **Craftsmanship over shortcuts** | "When did you refuse to ship/hand off work that wasn't right?" | Held the standard under deadline | "Good enough" under pressure |
| **The customer's moment** | "Describe a time you slowed down for an anxious customer." | Patience, empathy, no pressure | Transactional, close-focused |
| **Do right by earth & people** | "A time you raised an ethics/sourcing concern." | Spoke up, escalated | Looked away |
| **Earn trust again** | "How did you recover a relationship after a mistake?" | Owned it, made it right | Blames others |
| **Precision** | "A time a small detail mattered a lot." | Systematic, double-checks | Careless with details |

## 8. Candidate experience

How we treat candidates is how we treat customers — it's the same brand and the same values. Every candidate, hired or not, should leave with respect for Palencia.

- **Timely, honest communication.** Tell candidates the process and timeline up front, and keep to it. No ghosting — everyone who interviews gets a real response.
- **Prepared, respectful interviews.** Interviewers read the résumé, show up on time, and never waste a candidate's craft assessment.
- **Fair pay conversations.** Share the range honestly and early where practical or required.
- **Kind rejections.** Decline with warmth and, where useful and lawful, brief, usable feedback.
- **Reasonable accommodations** in the process for any candidate who needs them. `[bracketed] TODO: Confirm accommodation obligations per jurisdiction with counsel.`

## 9. Internal mobility and promotions

Great hires sometimes already work here. We post meaningful openings internally and support employees growing into new roles and departments.

- Internal candidates go through a fair, values-consistent version of the same process.
- Managers support development toward the next role — see the career ladders in [Training & Development](training-and-development.md) and the process in [Performance Management](performance-management.md).
- Craft progression (apprentice → setter → master; GG credential path) is a recognized route to advancement, not just external hiring.

## 11. The 30 / 60 / 90-day onboarding plan

Onboarding isn't paperwork — it's how someone becomes trusted with our craft and our inventory. Structure it around clear milestones.

| Window | Theme | Goals |
|---|---|---|
| **First 30 days — Learn** | Foundations & trust | Understand mission and values in practice; complete required compliance and security training (see [Training & Development](training-and-development.md)); shadow the team; learn systems; earn appropriate, least-privilege access. Manager 1:1s weekly. |
| **Days 31–60 — Contribute** | Supervised ownership | Own real work with a safety net: setters set under review; CX handles real conversations with coaching; gemologists grade with QC sign-off. First feedback checkpoint against the 90-day success definition. |
| **Days 61–90 — Own** | Independence | Perform core role responsibilities independently to standard; full (still least-privilege) system/inventory access as warranted; formal 90-day review with the manager. Confirm the hire is on track and reset goals for the next quarter. |

## 13. Hiring metrics we watch

We measure hiring the way we measure everything else — honestly, to improve.

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Quality of hire** | Are new hires meeting the 90-day success definition and living the values? |
| **Time to hire** | Are we respectful of candidates' time and moving decisively? |
| **Offer acceptance rate** | Is our pitch and comp honest and competitive? |
| **New-hire retention (first year)** | Did we scope and select well? |
| **Diversity of pipeline** | Are we sourcing widely and fairly? |
| **Candidate experience** | Would candidates (even declined ones) recommend the process? |
