## 1. Our philosophy

Performance management at Palencia is not a scoring exercise — it's how we help good people become excellent and keep the whole team pointed at the same goal: **being the most trusted name in fine diamond jewelry.**

Four commitments:
1. **No surprises.** By review time, nothing should be new. Feedback is continuous, not annual.
2. **What *and* how.** We evaluate results **and** how they were achieved — living the [values](../00-company/mission-vision-values.md) is part of performance, not separate from it.
3. **Honest and kind.** The same candor we owe customers, we owe each other — delivered with respect.
4. **Growth-oriented.** The goal is a better colleague next quarter, not a verdict on the last one.

## 2. Goal-setting

Goals connect each person's work to their department's KPIs and the company mission. We set them quarterly and revisit them in every check-in.

**Good goals are:** specific, measurable, owned by the individual, tied to a department KPI, and time-bound.

| Function | Illustrative KPI-linked goals |
|---|---|
| **Gemology** | Grading accuracy vs. lab reconciliation; turnaround on intake verification; zero certificate/stone mismatches. |
| **Jeweler / Setter** | First-pass QC rate; on-time completion; rework rate; craft-progression milestone. |
| **Quality Control** | Defect escape rate (post-ship issues); inspection throughput; documentation completeness. |
| **Sales / CX** | Customer satisfaction / would-recommend rate; response-time SLA; honest-advice quality (via review); return-for-fit vs. return-for-dissatisfaction. |
| **Marketing** | Qualified demand; content/SEO outcomes; brand-consistency. |
| **Operations** | Fulfillment accuracy; on-time insured shipment; inventory reconciliation accuracy. |
| **Finance / Compliance** | Controls adherence; AML/KYC timeliness and accuracy; audit findings closed. |

Each person carries at least one **values or growth goal** alongside output goals (e.g., complete a GIA course, mentor an apprentice, improve a process).

## 3. Review cadence

Frequent, lightweight, and forward-looking beats a single dreaded annual event.

| Rhythm | Frequency | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **1:1s** | Weekly / biweekly | Unblock, coach, and give real-time feedback. Not a status meeting. |
| **Quarterly check-in** | Every 3 months | Progress vs. goals, competency growth, reset next quarter's goals. Lightweight, documented. |
| **Annual review** | Yearly | Full look-back on results + values/competencies, career direction, and input to compensation review. |
| **Continuous feedback** | Always | Praise and course-corrections in the moment; peer feedback welcomed. |

## 4. Competency framework

We assess growth against competencies that combine craft, contribution, and values. Ratings use a common scale so expectations are consistent across the company.

**Rating scale:** 1 = Developing · 2 = Meets · 3 = Strong · 4 = Role model

| Competency | What "Strong" looks like |
|---|---|
| **Craft & expertise** | Deep, current skill in the role; work is a reference standard others learn from. |
| **Quality & precision** | Consistently accurate; catches errors before they ship; documents thoroughly. |
| **Customer & trust** | Puts the customer's moment first; builds trust that generates referrals. |
| **Honesty & integrity** | Tells hard truths; escalates concerns; never shades a grade, price, or timeline. |
| **Reliability & ownership** | Owns outcomes end to end; delivers on commitments; owns mistakes and fixes them. |
| **Collaboration** | Makes the team better; shares knowledge; handles handoffs cleanly. |
| **Security & compliance** | Rigorous with chain of custody, data, and AML/sourcing duties. |
| **Growth & adaptability** | Seeks feedback, learns fast, adapts to change. |
| **Leadership** *(as applicable)* | Develops others; sets and holds the standard; represents the Palencia name well. |

## 5. Values-based evaluation

Half of "how someone is doing" is *how* they work. Each review explicitly rates the six values.

| Value | Evaluation question |
|---|---|
| **Honesty is the product** | Do they tell customers and colleagues the truth, even when it's costly? |
| **Craftsmanship over shortcuts** | Do they hold the quality bar under deadline pressure? |
| **The customer's moment** | Do they lead with the customer's experience, not the transaction? |
| **Do right by earth & people** | Do they take sourcing and ethics seriously and speak up? |
| **Earn trust again** | Do they recover from mistakes with ownership and care? |
| **Precision in everything** | Are they systematic, accurate, and thorough? |

A serious values breach (e.g., dishonesty about a grade, a security shortcut, retaliation) outweighs strong output. Excellent results achieved by violating the values are **not** a good performance outcome.

## 6. Feedback norms

- **Timely.** Give it close to the event, while it's useful.
- **Specific & behavioral.** Describe what happened and its impact, not character labels.
- **Balanced.** Name strengths honestly, not just gaps — recognition is feedback too.
- **Two-way.** Managers ask for feedback on themselves and act on it.
- **Documented for reviews.** Keep light notes through the quarter so reviews are evidence-based, not recency-biased.

**A simple structure:** *Situation → Behavior → Impact → Ask.* ("In yesterday's consult (S), you walked the customer through why the better-cut stone suited their budget (B), which built real trust and they referred a friend (I). Keep doing that on every call (A).")

## 9. Performance improvement — done with care

When someone isn't meeting expectations, we treat it as a problem to solve *together*, early and honestly — not a gotcha.

1. **Early, direct conversation.** Name the gap specifically and check for blockers (unclear expectations, training, personal circumstances, tooling).
2. **Support first.** Coaching, training (see [Training & Development](training-and-development.md)), mentorship, or role adjustment where appropriate.
3. **Written improvement plan** if the gap persists: specific, measurable goals; the support provided; a fair timeframe (`[bracketed] TODO: e.g., 30–90 days`); and clear check-ins.
4. **Regular check-ins** with honest progress feedback throughout — no surprise outcomes.
5. **Outcome.** Success → back on track and recognized. Insufficient progress → next steps handled respectfully and lawfully.

> **Serious misconduct** (theft, fraud, security or safety violations, harassment, dishonesty about a stone or price) follows the [Code of Conduct](../02-governance/code-of-conduct.md) disciplinary process, not the improvement-plan path, and may warrant immediate action. `[bracketed] TODO: All discipline and termination steps — documentation, notice, final pay, appeals — MUST be reviewed with employment counsel for the relevant jurisdiction.`

Use this for quarterly and annual reviews. Keep it evidence-based and conversational.

```
DATE: ______________   TYPE: [ ] Quarterly  [ ] Annual

1. GOALS FROM LAST PERIOD
   Goal (linked KPI)            | Outcome        | Rating (1–4)
   ----------------------------- | -------------- | -----------
   ...                          | ...            | ...

2. COMPETENCIES (1 Developing · 2 Meets · 3 Strong · 4 Role model)
   Craft & expertise ___   Quality & precision ___   Customer & trust ___
   Honesty & integrity ___ Reliability & ownership ___ Collaboration ___
   Security & compliance ___ Growth & adaptability ___ Leadership ___ (if applicable)

3. VALUES IN PRACTICE
   Highlight where the six values showed up (with examples): ____________

4. STRENGTHS (specific, with impact): ____________________________

5. GROWTH AREAS (specific, with support offered): ________________

6. GOALS FOR NEXT PERIOD (specific, measurable, KPI-linked): _____
   Include at least one values/development goal.

7. CAREER & DEVELOPMENT (aspirations, training, ladder step): ____

8. EMPLOYEE COMMENTS (their voice matters — two-way): ___________

Manager signature: __________   Employee signature: __________
(Signature acknowledges the conversation, not necessarily agreement.)
```
