## 1. Why this document exists

"Honest prices" is one of our three brand promises. That is only credible if our pricing is built the same way every time, from real inputs a customer could — in principle — verify. This document defines:

- The **cost build-up** for a piece of jewelry, line by line.
- Our **margin philosophy** and how it differs from traditional retail markup.
- How we **price fairly but sustainably**, and how we **handle volatile inputs** (gold, platinum, diamond price shifts).
- How we price **custom design, deposits, and financing**.
- Our **promotions philosophy** — why we avoid fake-discount theater.

The goal: any price we quote should be one we can explain out loud, to a customer, without flinching.

## 2. Pricing principles

| # | Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Build up, don't mark up** | Price starts from real costs and a disclosed margin — not a mystery multiple of a "manufacturer's suggested" number. |
| 2 | **Same method, every piece** | The cost build-up in §3 applies to catalog and custom alike. No ad-hoc pricing. |
| 3 | **The certificate is priced, not the story** | We price the stone the GIA/IGI report describes. We do not charge for grade inflation or sentiment. |
| 4 | **Margin funds service, not theater** | Our margin pays for certification, hand-setting, QC, insured shipping, resizing, and warranty — real value, not markup for markup's sake. |
| 5 | **No fake anchors** | We do not invent inflated "compare-at" prices to manufacture a discount. See §10. |
| 6 | **Volatile inputs get rules, not guesses** | Metal and stone costs move; §8 defines how we reprice so we never sell below cost or gouge on a spike. |

## 3. The cost build-up

Every piece is priced from the same stack. We compute **landed cost**, apply a **margin**, then round to a clean retail price.

| Layer | What it captures | How we source the number |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Stone cost** | Center stone + any accent/melee diamonds or gemstones | Actual supplier invoice per stone; melee by carat lot. See [Sourcing & Procurement](../03-operations/sourcing-and-procurement.md). |
| **2. Metal cost** | Precious metal by weight × spot price × purity factor | Finished-piece metal weight (g) × current spot × purity (see §4) + refining/loss allowance |
| **3. Labor & setting** | Casting, hand-setting, finishing, engraving | Standard labor rate per setting type (see §6); custom quoted per hours |
| **4. Certification & grading** | GIA/IGI report cost already embedded in stone; any re-cert or appraisal | Pass-through at cost |
| **5. Findings & components** | Clasps, posts, backs, chain, prongs/heads not in metal weight | At cost from components schedule |
| **6. Inbound & QC** | Insured inbound freight, receiving inspection, QC time | Allocated per piece (overhead pool, §7) |
| **7. Overhead allocation** | Rent, software/platform, payment processing, insurance, packaging, team | Allocated as a % of landed cost or per-unit (see §7) |
| = | **Landed cost** | Sum of layers 1–7 |
| **8. Margin** | Gross margin to fund service promises + profit | Landed cost ÷ (1 − target margin %) — see §5 |
| = | **List price** | Rounded per §9 rounding rules |

## 5. Margin philosophy — fair yet sustainable

We target **gross margin**, not keystone markup. Keystone (2×+ cost) and the "5–10× markup" common in big-box diamond retail are exactly the markup theater we reject.

- **Margin is disclosed in principle, not hidden.** We can defend every price against its build-up.
- **Margin covers real obligations:** certification, hand-setting, QC, free insured shipping, resizing, warranty, returns, financing cost, and payment processing — plus sustainable profit to keep the company healthy (see [Financial Controls](financial-controls.md) for how that flows to the P&L).
- **Higher-value stones carry lower percentage margin.** A $12,000 center stone does not carry the same margin *percentage* as a $400 pair of studs — dollar margin, not multiple, is what scales.

## 14. Promotions philosophy — no fake-discount theater

Traditional jewelry retail runs on perpetual "50% off" sales anchored to prices no one ever paid. That is the opposite of honest pricing, and in many jurisdictions inflated "reference prices" are unlawful.

**What we do:**

- Price fairly **every day**; the everyday price *is* the offer.
- If we run a genuine promotion, the "compare-at" is a real, previously-charged price we actually sold at — never an invented anchor.
- Prefer **value-adds** (e.g. complimentary engraving, extended warranty, a gift) over price-slashing that undermines the "honest price" credibility.
- Seasonal or clearance markdowns on aging inventory are fine and honest — they reflect real inventory decisions, disclosed plainly.

**What we never do:**

- Invent an inflated MSRP/"was" price to manufacture a discount.
- Run a "limited time" sale that is actually permanent.
- Raise a price before a "sale" to fake a markdown.
