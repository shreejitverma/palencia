## 1. Why this matters

High-value, portable, easily resold goods — diamonds, gold, platinum, and fine jewelry — are attractive vehicles for laundering illicit funds and evading sanctions. Regulators treat dealers in precious stones/metals/jewels as a risk category. A single mishandled cash transaction or missed sanctions hit can expose the company to penalties and reputational harm. Our brand is built on trust; AML compliance protects it.

## 3. Risk-based approach

We calibrate effort to risk. Every customer, product, channel, and geography is assessed on a risk spectrum, and higher risk triggers enhanced measures.

| Risk factor | Lower risk | Higher risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Payment** | Card, bank transfer from named account | Cash, cash-equivalents, third-party or structured payments |
| **Customer** | Verified retail buyer, consistent profile | Anonymity attempts, PEPs, opaque entities, mismatched profile |
| **Geography** | Domestic, low-risk jurisdiction | Sanctioned/high-risk (CAHRA) countries, shipping mismatch |
| **Product** | Single retail piece | High-value loose stones, bulk, easily-resold generic goods |
| **Channel** | Standard e-commerce checkout | Off-platform, in-person cash, trade/wholesale |

The program has three intensity levels: **Standard Due Diligence**, **Simplified** (documented low risk), and **Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)** for high-risk cases.

## 5. Cash-transaction reporting (US Form 8300)

## 7. Jewelry-specific red flags

| # | Red flag | Why it matters | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer offers to pay in cash near/above the reporting threshold | Classic placement of illicit funds | EDD; consider Form 8300; may decline |
| 2 | Requests to split payment to stay under thresholds (structuring) | Evasion of reporting | **Refuse**; escalate to Compliance Officer; consider SAR/STR |
| 3 | Indifference to price, quality, size, or the 4Cs | Buyer values the asset only as a value store | EDD |
| 4 | Buys highly generic, easily-resold high-value items in bulk | Layering / resale for laundering | EDD |
| 5 | Third party pays, or payment comes from an unrelated account | Obscures source of funds | Verify relationship & source of funds |
| 6 | Reluctance to provide ID or beneficial-ownership info | Anonymity-seeking | EDD or decline |
| 7 | Ship-to, bill-to, and IP/geolocation don't reconcile | Possible diversion / sanctions evasion | Verify; sanctions re-screen |
| 8 | Overpayment then request for refund to a different method/account | Refund fraud / laundering | Refund only to original method; escalate |
| 9 | Sanctioned-jurisdiction nexus or name match | Sanctions violation risk (strict liability) | **Stop**; escalate before proceeding |
| 10 | Rapid buy-and-return or buy-and-resell patterns | Layering | Monitor; EDD |
| 11 | Nervous, evasive, or coached-sounding answers about source of funds | Concealment | EDD |
| 12 | Trade buyer with no verifiable business footprint | Shell/front risk | KYB before dealing |

> Red flags are signals to investigate, not automatic accusations. Document what you saw and what you did.

## 8. Sanctions & OFAC screening

- **Who we screen:** Customers, trade buyers, beneficial owners, suppliers, and — where feasible — ship-to recipients, against OFAC SDN and relevant consolidated sanctions lists. `[TODO: confirm screening tool/vendor and which additional lists (EU/UK/UN) apply.]`
- **When:** At onboarding, before high-value or high-risk transactions, and on list updates.
- **Match handling:** A potential match **pauses** the transaction. Do not proceed, tip off, or ship. Escalate to the Compliance Officer, who resolves false positives and handles true matches (blocking/reporting) per counsel.
- **Strict liability:** Sanctions violations do not require intent — screening is mandatory, not discretionary.

## 12. Employee training

- All customer-facing, finance, and sourcing staff complete AML/KYC training at onboarding and at least **annually**.

### Frontline quick-reference checklist

- [ ] Does the payment method or amount raise a cash/structuring flag?
