## 1. Purpose & scope

Custom design is one of Palencia Diamonds' defining offers: a made-to-order piece with the customer's choice of **material, stone, cut, and carat**. This document governs every bespoke commission from consultation to the point the finished piece enters [Order Fulfillment](order-fulfillment.md) for packaging and dispatch.

It protects three things:

1. **The customer's vision** — captured precisely, approved at every irreversible step, never assumed.
2. **The brand promise** — certified stones, hand-set craftsmanship, honest prices, delivered on an honest timeline.
3. **The business** — clear deposits, a fair revision policy, and no ambiguity about what was agreed.

> A custom piece is emotional and irreversible once cast and set. **We approve before we commit material.** Every gate below is a "measure twice, cut once" checkpoint.

> This is an operational reference, **not legal advice.** Deposit, refund, and contract terms must be reviewed by qualified counsel for the jurisdictions we serve.

## 3. Milestone gates at a glance

Each **gate** requires an explicit action before work continues. Customer-approval gates require the customer's **written** sign-off (email or portal confirmation) — never verbal only.

| 2 | Budget & stone selection | Customer | ✅ Stone & budget agreed |
| 3 | Deposit & quote acceptance | Customer + Finance | ✅ Quote accepted, deposit paid |
| 4 | CAD design & rendering | Customer | ✅ Render approved |
| 5 | Wax / 3D-printed model | Customer | ✅ Model approved |
| 6 | Casting | Internal (irreversible) | — |
| 7 | Stone setting | Internal | — |
| 8 | Finishing & polish | Internal | — |
| 9 | Quality Control | Internal (hard gate) | — |

> **Irreversibility line:** After Gate 5 (model approval) we begin committing material at Gate 6 (casting). No customer changes to form or size after Gate 5 without a new quote and revised timeline.

## 5. Gate 1 — Consultation & brief capture

**Goal:** Understand the person and the moment before the piece.

1. Book the consultation (video, phone, or in-person). Approach with patience and zero pressure — this is the customer's moment, not our transaction.
2. Capture the **brief** in the commission record:
   - Occasion and recipient; any deadline (e.g., a proposal or anniversary date).
   - Style direction: references, inspiration images, existing pieces, dislikes.
   - Design type: engagement ring, band, pendant, earrings, etc.
   - Metal preference: yellow / white / rose gold or platinum.
   - Center-stone direction: shape/cut, target carat, and quality priorities.
   - Practical needs: ring size, wearability, budget range.
3. Confirm the brief back to the customer in writing before proceeding.

**Brief checklist**

- [ ] Occasion, recipient, and any hard deadline
- [ ] Style references captured
- [ ] Metal preference
- [ ] Stone direction (shape, target carat, quality priorities)
- [ ] Size and wearability needs
- [ ] Budget range agreed
- [ ] Brief confirmed back in writing

## 6. Gate 2 — Budget & stone selection

**Goal:** Choose the certified center stone and lock the budget before design.

1. Present stone options within budget, balancing the **4Cs** against the customer's stated priorities honestly — including the trade-offs (e.g., a slightly lower color for a larger carat).
2. Every option is independently **GIA or IGI** certified. Share the grading report and, where present, the laser-inscription reference.
3. Confirm responsible-sourcing warranties (Kimberley Process / RJC) for the chosen stone.
4. Reserve the selected stone against the commission (coordinate with [Inventory Management](inventory-management.md) if in stock, or source per [Order Fulfillment](order-fulfillment.md) Stage 3).
5. Confirm metal and any accent-stone choices.

> **Honesty gate:** Never oversell a grade to hit a look. State exactly what the customer is buying and why it fits the budget.

## 7. Gate 3 — Quote & deposit

**Goal:** Agree price and secure commitment before design labor begins.

1. Prepare a transparent quote: stone, metal, setting/labor, engraving, and any rush.
2. Present the **payment schedule** (below) and the **revision policy** (Section 12).
3. On acceptance, Finance collects the **deposit**; timeline and promise date are confirmed from this point.

## 8. Gate 4 — CAD design & rendering

**Goal:** Show the customer their piece before any material is committed.

1. CAD Designer models the piece to the brief and stone spec.
2. Produce photorealistic renders from multiple angles; confirm proportions, gallery, prongs, band width, and profile.
3. Send renders for review. Log each revision round against the revision policy.
4. **Gate:** Customer approves the render in writing. Approval confirms form, proportions, metal, and stone placement.

## 9. Gate 5 — Wax / 3D-printed model

**Goal:** Let the customer approve the piece in three dimensions.

1. Print a resin/wax model (and share photos/video, or an in-person/mailed model where offered).
2. Customer reviews physical form, size, and feel.
3. **Gate:** Customer approves the model in writing.

> **This is the last change point for form and size.** After model approval, form/size changes require a new quote and schedule.

## 10. Gates 6–8 — Casting, setting & finishing

1. **Casting (Gate 6):** Cast the approved model in the chosen metal. Confirm hallmark/metal stamp for metal and jurisdiction. *Irreversible — material is now committed.*
2. **Stone setting (Gate 7):** Hand-set the certified center stone and any accents. Verify the stone against its certificate before and after setting.
3. **Finishing (Gate 8):** File, polish, and finish to Palencia standard. Apply engraving if commissioned (verify text character-for-character — see [Order Fulfillment](order-fulfillment.md) Stage 5).

## 11. Gates 9–11 — QC, final approval & delivery

1. **Quality Control (Gate 9):** Independent QC inspects against the approved renders and the order — stone-to-certificate match, secure setting, correct metal/hallmark/size, engraving, and finish. Hard gate; nothing advances on a fail. See [Quality Control](quality-control.md).
2. **Final approval (Gate 10):** Send final photos (and offer a review call). Collect the balance payment.
3. **Delivery (Gate 11):** The finished piece enters [Order Fulfillment](order-fulfillment.md) at its **Stage 7/8** — packaged with the GIA/IGI certificate, appraisal, and care card, then shipped insured and signature-confirmed per [Shipping & Insurance](shipping-and-insurance.md).

## 14. Exceptions

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Customer changes form after Gate 5 | New quote + revised timeline; document agreement |
| Sourced stone unavailable | Re-present alternatives within budget; adjust schedule |
| Deadline at risk | Proactive outreach **before** the date; offer rush options if feasible |
| Customer unresponsive at a gate | Pause the clock; commission cannot advance without written approval |
| QC fail | Return to bench; update customer if the promise date is affected |
