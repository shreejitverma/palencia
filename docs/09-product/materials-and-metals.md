## 1. Why this matters

Metal is half of every piece we sell. It sets the color, the weight, the durability, a large share of the price, and — for some customers — whether they can wear it comfortably at all. Describing it precisely is part of *"Certified diamonds. Handcrafted jewelry. Honest prices."* This document is the shared reference so Merchandising, Production, QC, Sales, and Content all say the same true things about our materials.

## 2. Gold — the fundamentals

Pure gold (**24k**) is beautiful and never tarnishes, but it is **too soft** for everyday jewelry — it bends, scratches, and loses detail. So gold is **alloyed** (mixed) with other metals for strength, durability, and color. **Karat (k)** measures gold purity in 24ths.

### 2.1 Karat reference

| Karat | Gold content | Parts gold / 24 | Marking | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **10k** | 41.7% | 10/24 | `10K` / `417` | Hardest & most affordable; palest yellow; more alloy |
| **14k** | 58.3% | 14/24 | `14K` / `585` | **Our workhorse** — ideal balance of durability, color, value; best for daily wear |
| **18k** | 75.0% | 18/24 | `18K` / `750` | Rich, warm color; more precious; softer than 14k |
| **22k** | 91.7% | 22/24 | `22K` / `916` | Deep, saturated gold; soft — better for lighter-wear pieces than daily rings |
| **24k** | 99.9% | 24/24 | `24K` / `999` | Pure; too soft for fine jewelry — not used for our set pieces |

> **The trade-off in one line:** Higher karat = richer color and more precious metal, but **softer**. Lower karat = more durable and affordable, but paler and more alloy. **14k** wins for everyday rings; **18k** for a more luxurious look and feel.

### 2.2 The three gold colors

Gold's color comes from **what it's alloyed with**. The yellow you see is real gold; white and rose are the same gold alloyed differently.

| Color | Typical alloy additions | Look | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yellow gold** | Copper + silver (+ zinc) | Classic warm gold | Naturally hides warmth in lower-color diamonds; timeless; no plating |
| **White gold** | Palladium/nickel + others; then **rhodium-plated** | Bright silvery-white | Plating gives the icy finish; **re-plating is normal maintenance** (see §2.3) |
| **Rose gold** | Higher **copper** content | Warm pink/rose | Copper makes it durable; color deepens with more copper; on-trend, romantic |

> **Color perception tip:** A warmer diamond (I–K) looks beautifully white set in **yellow or rose gold** because the metal masks warmth. For an icy look, pair higher-color stones (G–H+) with **white gold or platinum**. Cross-reference [Diamond Education — Color](diamond-education-4cs.md#4-color--the-absence-of-color).

### 2.3 White gold & rhodium plating

White gold isn't naturally bright white — it carries a faint warm or grayish tone from the gold. To achieve the crisp, mirror-white finish, white gold is **plated with rhodium**, a hard, brilliant white platinum-group metal.

- **Rhodium plating wears over time** (months to a few years depending on wear, skin chemistry, and contact). As it thins, a slight warmth may show through — this is **normal, not a defect**.
- **Re-plating restores the finish.** We offer/advise periodic re-plating as routine maintenance. See [Returns, Warranty & Resizing](../04-sales-cx/returns-warranty-resizing.md).
- **Honest disclosure:** We tell customers up front that white gold is rhodium-plated and will need occasional re-plating. Customers who want a permanently white metal with **no plating** should consider **platinum**.

## 3. Platinum — the premium metal

Platinum is a naturally **white**, dense, and highly durable precious metal. It's used at much higher purity than gold and never needs plating to stay white.

| Attribute | Platinum | 14k/18k white gold |
|---|---|---|
| **Purity (our standard)** | 90–95% pure (`PT950` / `PLAT`) | 58.3% / 75% gold |
| **Color** | Naturally white — **permanent** | White via **rhodium plating** (needs upkeep) |
| **Density / weight** | Denser — feels noticeably heavier | Lighter |
| **Durability** | Extremely durable; doesn't wear away | Durable; plating wears |
| **Scratches** | Develops a soft **patina**; metal is displaced, not lost | Scratches remove plating/metal |
| **Hypoallergenic** | Excellent — naturally hypoallergenic | Good, unless nickel-containing (see §5) |
| **Price** | **Premium** — higher purity + density + labor | Lower |

### 3.1 Why platinum costs more

Platinum is priced higher than equivalent white gold because: (1) it's used at **~95% purity** vs. 58–75% for gold, so there's more precious metal; (2) it's **denser**, so a piece contains more grams; (3) it's harder to work, requiring more skilled labor. The payoff: a naturally white metal that **never needs plating** and holds a stone securely for a lifetime.

> **Patina, not damage:** Over time platinum develops a soft, matte patina from micro-scratches. Many customers love this antique look; others prefer it polished back to high shine. Either is easy — set expectations honestly.

### 3.2 Purity marks

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PT950` / `950PT` / `PLAT` | 95% platinum (our standard for fine jewelry) |
| `PT900` | 90% platinum |
| `IRIDPLAT` | Older mark: platinum alloyed with iridium |

## 5. Durability & hypoallergenic notes

- **Nickel allergy** is the most common metal sensitivity. Some **white gold** alloys use nickel to whiten; a small share of wearers react (redness, itching). **Nickel-safe options:** platinum, palladium-white gold, or yellow/rose gold. For sensitive customers we recommend **platinum** or a **nickel-free white gold**, and we disclose alloy content on request.
- **Copper** (in rose gold and as a gold alloy) can, in rare cases, cause slight skin discoloration in some individuals — cosmetic and harmless.
- **Higher karat = softer.** An 18k prong wears faster than a 14k prong. For very active wearers, 14k or platinum prongs hold stones more securely over time.
- **Platinum prongs** are prized for security: the metal moves rather than wears away, so it retains grip on a stone longer.

## 6. Metal weight, spot price & pricing

The **precious-metal value** of a piece is roughly its metal weight × purity × the daily **spot price**, plus alloys, labor, and design. Because spot prices move daily, metal weight (captured per product — see [Product Taxonomy §5](product-taxonomy.md#51-core-attributes-all-products)) is a required attribute.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Spot price** | Live market price of pure gold/platinum, quoted per troy ounce |
| **Troy ounce** | Precious-metal weight unit: 1 troy oz ≈ 31.103 g |
| **Fine/pure content** | Metal weight × purity (e.g., 4 g of 14k = 4 × 0.583 ≈ 2.33 g fine gold) |
| **Metal value** | Fine content × spot (a *floor* input to price, not the retail price) |

> Metal cost is one **transparent input** to price alongside stones, craftsmanship, and service — see [Pricing Strategy](../08-finance/pricing-strategy.md). We can always explain *why* a piece costs what it does; that's the "honest prices" promise.

## 7. Hallmarking & stamping standards

A **hallmark** (quality stamp) tells the wearer what the metal is. In the U.S., the **National Gold & Silver Stamping Act** requires that a karat/quality mark be accompanied by a **maker's or registered trademark** so the mark is accountable to someone. We stamp accordingly.

| Element | Example | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality mark** | `14K`, `585`, `750`, `PT950` | States the metal/purity |
| **Maker's mark / trademark** | Palencia registered mark | Identifies who stands behind the quality mark (legally required alongside it) |
| **Tolerance** | Plumb-marked to standard | U.S. karat marks must meet the stated fineness within legal tolerance |

- Marks are placed discreetly — typically the **inner shank** of a ring, the **clasp or tag** of a chain/bracelet, or the **post/back** of an earring.
- Lab-grown center stones also carry a **girdle inscription** (see [Certification & Grading](../05-compliance-ethics/certification-and-grading.md)); that's a stone mark, distinct from the metal hallmark.

## 8. Gemstone settings & prongs

The **setting** holds the stone. Setting choice affects security, how much light reaches the stone, and the look.

| Setting | How it holds the stone | Security | Light / look | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Prong** (4 or 6) | Metal claws grip the stone | Good; exposed tips can snag/wear | Maximum light & sparkle | Solitaires, studs |
| **Bezel** | Metal rim fully/partly surrounds the stone | Highest | Less light in; modern, protective | Active lifestyles |
| **Halo** | Small stones ring the center | Good (adds protection to girdle) | Adds sparkle & apparent size | Halo/hidden-halo rings |
| **Pavé** | Tiny beads/prongs set many small stones | Good with quality work | Continuous sparkle on the band | Pavé bands, accents |
| **Channel** | Stones set in a groove between two walls | High; no exposed prongs | Sleek, protected | Eternity/wedding bands |
| **Tension (look)** | Stone appears held by pressure | Varies | Contemporary | Statement pieces |

- **6-prong** spreads grip and is a classic secure choice for rounds; **4-prong** shows more of the stone and can make it look slightly squarer/larger.
- **Prong metal matters:** platinum and 14k prongs resist wear better than 18k for long-term security (see §5).
- QC checks every setting for tight, even prongs and no sharp edges before a piece ships — see [Quality Control](../03-operations/quality-control.md).

## 9. Jewelry care & cleaning

Honest care advice keeps pieces beautiful and reduces avoidable damage and returns. Share the relevant guidance at point of sale and in the care card.

### 9.1 General principles

- **Take it off** for cleaning with harsh chemicals, swimming (chlorine attacks alloys, especially gold solder joints), gym/weights, gardening, and heavy manual work.
- **Last on, first off:** put jewelry on **after** lotion, perfume, sunscreen, and hairspray; remove it first when doing rough work.
- **Store separately** — diamonds scratch other gems and metals. Use pouches or a lined box; don't pile pieces together.
- **Routine clean:** soak in **warm water + a little mild dish soap**, brush gently with a **soft** toothbrush behind the stone (where oils/lotion dull sparkle), rinse, pat dry with a lint-free cloth.

### 9.2 Care by metal

| Metal | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Yellow / rose gold** | Warm soapy water; soft cloth polish | Chlorine (weakens over time); abrasive cleaners |
| **White gold** | Gentle cleaning; **periodic rhodium re-plating** to renew whiteness | Harsh scrubbing that strips plating faster |
| **Platinum** | Warm soapy water; professional polish to remove patina if desired | Nothing special — very forgiving; patina is normal |
| **All + pavé/many small stones** | Soft brush, gentle rinse | High-pressure or careless brushing that could loosen melee |

### 9.3 Care by piece type

| Piece | Extra care |
|---|---|
| **Engagement rings** | Clean weekly (skin oils dull diamonds fastest); check prongs periodically |
| **Tennis necklaces/bracelets** | Inspect clasp and links; the flexible line takes stress — professional check yearly |
| **Stud & drop earrings** | Check backs regularly; clean posts; store as a pair |
| **Chains** | Store hung or flat to prevent kinks; clean gently to reach links |
| **Eternity/channel bands** | Not always resizable (stones all around) — advise on sizing at purchase |

### 9.4 Professional service

- **Ultrasonic/steam cleaning** is effective but **not for every stone or setting** — fracture-filled, heavily included, or certain gem materials can be harmed. We use it selectively and advise customers accordingly.
- **Prong tightening, re-plating, resizing, and refinishing** are periodic services — see [Returns, Warranty & Resizing](../04-sales-cx/returns-warranty-resizing.md).
